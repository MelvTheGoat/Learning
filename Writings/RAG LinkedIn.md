# What this post has to do differently from the long-form piece

LinkedIn is not a teaser for the real post — most people who read it will never click through, so it has to be a complete, satisfying read on its own. Its only two jobs: make someone who scrolls past in three seconds actually stop, and make someone who reads the whole thing want to see the rest. Target length: 150–250 words. Anything longer gets truncated behind "see more" and loses most of your audience right there.

No headers, no bullets-as-structure the way a blog post would use them — LinkedIn's plain-text feed format rewards short paragraphs and line breaks, not markdown formatting.

---

## 1. The first two lines — before "see more" cuts it off

This is the only part guaranteed to be seen by everyone who scrolls past. It has to work as a complete thought on its own, not a lead-in. Use the same real moment from the long-form hook — a compliance officer needing one specific answer and having no fast way to check it — compressed to 1–2 short lines. No "I built a RAG system" here; that's the least interesting sentence you could open with.

## 2. The problem, in three lines or fewer

Why the manual approach is genuinely painful — long, dense, scattered documents, and a real cost to getting it wrong. Keep it grounded in Nigeria/CBN/NDPA specifically; the specificity is what makes it credible instead of generic.

## 3. What you built, one line

The same one-sentence description from the long-form outline. What it does, who it's for, what makes an answer trustworthy.

## 4. One number, and one honest line about it

Pick the single most interesting thing from your evaluation work — not the flattering headline number alone, but paired with the moment of digging into something that looked bad and turning out to understand exactly why. This is the line that differentiates you from every other "I built an AI thing" post: most people post the win, you're posting the reasoning. One or two sentences, no more — this is a taste, not the full explanation.

## 5. It's live

One line. Deployed, running, real URL — not a repo you have to clone to try.

## 6. The link and the ask

Link to the full breakdown, framed as an invitation, not a demand: "wrote up the harder parts — chunking, refusal, the evaluation numbers — here: [link]." Optionally, a second link to the live demo itself if you want people trying it directly from LinkedIn rather than only reading about it.

## 7. Tags, used sparingly

3–5 relevant tags at the very end (#MachineLearning #RAG #Fintech or similar) — LinkedIn's own algorithm weighting means a handful of accurate tags help discovery; a wall of them looks like spam and can hurt reach.

---

# Before you write

**Decide the one thing you want a recruiter or hiring manager to remember** from a five-second read, and write toward that — not toward covering everything. The long-form post is where completeness lives; this post is where one sharp impression lives.

**Post it, then engage with your own comments for the first hour** if anyone asks a question — LinkedIn's algorithm rewards early engagement disproportionately, and answering a genuine question in the comments is free, credible follow-up content.
