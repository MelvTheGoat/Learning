# The actual question this document answers

Not "which country is cheapest" alone, and not "which country lets you work
the most hours" alone — those two numbers only mean something together.
Twenty-four work hours a week in an expensive city can leave you with less
spare cash than twenty hours a week somewhere tuition is near zero and rent
is a third of the price. Every country below is scored on the combination:
**tuition + realistic living cost, against realistic part-time income**,
so you can see where the actual gap sits, not just a headline number.

One honest framing before the numbers: "fund a large portion through work"
is realistic almost everywhere on this list for **living costs**. It is
only realistic for **tuition too** in the handful of countries where
tuition is near zero to begin with. That distinction drives the ranking
below.

# Tier 1 — Where the math genuinely works

## Germany — the strongest fit for your specific situation

**Tuition:** €0 at most public universities, even for non-EU students —
only a small semester administrative fee, typically €100–€400.

**Work rights:** non-EU students can work up to **140 full days or 280
half days per calendar year** (raised from 120/240 in 2026) — university
jobs (Hiwi positions) and mandatory course internships generally don't
count against this cap.

**Living cost:** roughly €800–€1,200/month depending on city — a smaller
city (Magdeburg, Dortmund) sits at the low end, Munich or Frankfurt at
the high end.

**Why this is your best option specifically:** with tuition already at
zero, part-time work only has to cover living costs, not tuition —
that's a fundamentally easier target than every other country here. A
student working steadily through the 140-day allowance at a typical
student-job wage can realistically cover most or all monthly living
costs in a mid-cost city. German public universities also weigh the
overall application (SOP, portfolio, sometimes an interview) more than
UK/US institutions do relative to raw GPA — which works in your favor
given your project portfolio.

**Universities to target** (English-taught MSc Data Science/Statistics,
realistic for your profile):
- **TU Dortmund** — Statistics department has real international
  reach and a strong quantitative reputation without the extreme
  selectivity of TU Munich.
- **Otto von Guericke University Magdeburg** — genuinely accessible,
  low cost of living, established MSc Data & Knowledge Engineering.
- **University of Mannheim** — stronger reputation, moderately more
  competitive, still realistic with a strong portfolio and SOP.
- **Technical University of Kaiserslautern-Landau** — solid, lower-profile,
  more accessible entry point.

**The one thing to check per program:** some German masters (especially
in Bavaria specifically) have started charging non-EU tuition at select
universities. Confirm "tuition-free for all nationalities" on the
specific program page, not just the country in general — this varies by
state (Bundesland), not just by university.

## Poland — cheapest all-in cost of living on this list

**Tuition:** English-taught Data Science/Statistics masters typically
**€2,000–€6,000/year** at public universities.

**Living cost:** the lowest on this list — **€400–€700/month** in
Kraków, Wrocław, or Poznań; student dormitories run €80–€150/month.

**Work rights:** full-time students generally face fewer bureaucratic
restrictions than in Western Europe, though the exact current hour
allowance should be confirmed directly with the specific voivodeship
labour office before you commit — don't rely on a blog's number here.

**Why this is genuinely strong for you:** the *combined* annual cost
(tuition + living) can land under €10,000/year — meaningfully lower than
UK or Canada — which means even modest part-time earnings close a much
larger share of the gap than the same earnings would in a pricier country.

**Universities to target:**
- **University of Warsaw** — strong, internationally recognized,
  Data Science MSc taught in English.
- **AGH University of Science and Technology (Kraków)** — genuinely
  strong in STEM, lower cost of living than Warsaw.
- **Warsaw University of Technology** — solid technical reputation,
  realistic admission bar.

## France — cheap public tuition, underrated for this specific goal

**Tuition:** public universities charge as little as **€243–€3,770/year**
even for non-EU masters students — genuinely one of the cheapest tuition
structures in Western Europe.

**Living cost:** high in Paris, meaningfully lower in Lyon, Toulouse, or
Nantes — budget for **€700–€1,000/month** outside the capital.

**Work rights:** non-EU students are generally permitted to work up to
roughly 60% of full-time hours annually (commonly cited as ~964 hours/year,
averaging to something close to 20 hours/week) — confirm the exact current
figure on the French immigration site (France-Visas) before relying on it.

**The catch:** most fully tuition-subsidized programs are taught in
French. English-taught Data Science/Statistics masters exist but are a
smaller pool — worth checking language requirements carefully per program
before applying.

**Universities to target:**
- **Université de Lille** — public tuition rates, established
  Data Science program, lower-cost city.
- **Université Toulouse III (Paul Sabatier)** — strong in applied
  statistics, cheaper city than Paris.
- **Université de Lyon** — public tuition, real tech and applied
  research sector locally.

# Tier 2 — Realistic, but tuition is the harder part to close with work alone

## Ireland — best combination of stay-back length and reasonable cost

**Tuition:** €9,000–€14,000/year at the more accessible universities
(DCU, University of Limerick), rising to €13,000–€20,000 at Trinity/UCD.

**Work rights:** 20 hours/week in term, **40 hours/week during official
holidays** (June–September) — a genuinely useful summer earning window.

**Living cost:** €900–€1,300/month, Dublin at the high end.

**The honest math:** part-time work can meaningfully offset living costs,
especially given the generous 40-hour summer window, but tuition itself
remains a real gap you'd likely need savings, a loan, or partial
scholarship to close — work alone won't cover both.

**Universities to target:** Dublin City University and University of
Limerick, both flagged in your original shortlist for GPA flexibility,
remain the right picks here specifically because they're also the more
affordable end of Irish tuition.

## Canada — strongest earning power, weakest tuition affordability

**Tuition:** rarely cheap — CAD 15,000–30,000/year is typical even at
less prestigious universities, though some regional and Quebec public
universities sit at the lower end of that range.

**Work rights:** **24 hours/week off-campus during term**, unlimited
during scheduled breaks — genuinely generous, and Canadian minimum wages
(commonly CAD 15–17/hour depending on province) make this a real income
source.

**Why it's Tier 2, not Tier 1, for your specific goal:** the work income
is real and can comfortably cover most living costs, but tuition itself
is high enough that "fund a large portion through work" is a harder claim
to make here than in Germany or Poland — you'd likely still need savings
or a loan for tuition specifically, with work covering the rest.

**Where to look for lower tuition specifically:** regional universities
outside Toronto/Vancouver — Quebec public universities (if
French-taught programs are viable for you) and Atlantic Canada
institutions (Memorial University of Newfoundland, University of New
Brunswick) tend to charge meaningfully less than the well-known names,
while still granting the identical 3-year PGWP.

## United Kingdom — least "cheap" of the realistic options

**Tuition:** typically £15,000–£25,000/year for a taught masters, even
at the more accessible universities from your original shortlist.

**Work rights:** 20 hours/week in term, full-time during holidays.

**The honest math:** at the National Living Wage (£12.71/hour), 20
hours/week for a full year comes to roughly £13,000 — a meaningful sum,
but it doesn't come close to closing a £20,000+ tuition bill on its own.
The UK remains a strong option **if** partial scholarship or savings
cover tuition and work covers living costs — it's a weaker fit
specifically for the "fund a large portion entirely through work" framing
you're asking about now.

# What I'd actually do

**Lead with Germany.** It's the only country here where your stated goal
— fund a large portion, ideally most of it, through work rather than
scholarship — is realistic for both tuition *and* living costs
simultaneously, not just one of the two. Combine it with Poland as a
genuine second option if a specific German program doesn't work out or
requires German-language proficiency you don't yet have.

**Apply to at least one Tier 1 country and one Tier 2 country in
parallel.** Germany and Poland as your primary targets; Ireland as a
backup where the numbers are harder but the post-study stay-back (24
months) is the most generous of any option here.

**Reconfirm every number above directly on the official source before
committing** — a country's official immigration site for work-hour rules,
the specific university's own fee page for tuition, not a blog. Several
of these figures (Germany's work-day cap, the UK's Graduate Route length)
have changed within the last two years, and getting a stale number wrong
here costs you real money.

**One structural point worth remembering:** the "initial payment" you
mentioned setting aside separately — deposit, visa proof-of-funds, first
semester — is unavoidable everywhere on this list, including Germany.
Proof-of-funds requirements specifically (Germany's blocked account is
currently around €11,904) exist precisely because immigration authorities
don't assume part-time work will cover you from day one. Budget for that
upfront number as a fixed cost regardless of which country you choose,
and treat everything above as the plan for what comes *after* you land.
