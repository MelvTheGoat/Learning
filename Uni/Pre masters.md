# Yes, this exists, and it's exactly what you described

You're describing a **Pre-Master's pathway** — a real, established route where
you enroll in a preparatory academic program, and passing it at a required
level with good attendance **guarantees** your place on the named master's
degree, at the same institution, with no separate re-application or fresh
admissions decision. One enrollment, two stages, one continuous outcome. This
is a mainstream, current option, not a workaround or a lesser-known trick.

Two structurally different versions of this exist, and the difference
matters for exactly what you asked ("a direct link, the pre-program and
program all together").

---

# The two real models

## Model A — Pathway provider programs (the closest match to what you want)

Companies like **Kaplan International Pathways**, **INTO University
Partnerships**, **Study Group**, and **Oxford International Education
Group** run dedicated study centres physically built on or immediately
next to a specific university's own campus. You apply once, to the
pathway program, naming your intended university and master's degree
upfront. Complete it at the required grade with acceptable attendance,
and **progression is guaranteed** — not a fresh application, a
confirmed continuation.

**Why this fits your exact ask:** it is genuinely one linked process from
the start. You know before you begin exactly which university and which
master's program you're heading into, and the pre-program's whole
curriculum is built specifically to prepare you for that named degree —
not a generic foundation year you then have to shop around with.

**Confirmed current example:** Kaplan's pathway into **Queen Mary
University of London** is described directly as giving guaranteed access
to QMUL's own master's programs — not a maybe, a guarantee conditional
only on your pass grade and attendance. Kaplan's current UK partner
list includes several Russell Group universities: **Queen Mary,
Glasgow, Nottingham, Birmingham, Bristol, Liverpool, and York.**

## Model B — University-direct pre-master's programs

A smaller number of universities run their **own** in-house pre-master's,
taught by their own staff, with the same guaranteed-progression structure
but no separate pathway company involved at all. **SOAS University of
London**, **UCL's Centre for Languages & International Education (CLIE)**,
and **QMUL's own direct pre-master's** are current, real examples.

**Why this might suit you even better:** no intermediary company, the
program is taught by the actual university, and in at least one case
(SOAS) it's available **fully online** — meaning you could complete the
whole pre-master's stage from Nigeria before ever needing a UK visa,
which is a real cost and logistics saving worth knowing about.

# How this specifically helps your GPA situation

This is the actual mechanism, worth being precise about: a pathway
provider's entry requirement for the pre-master's stage is meaningfully
lower than direct entry into the master's itself — it's built specifically
for applicants whose undergraduate result doesn't clear the university's
normal masters bar. **You are not applying to the master's with a 2.02/4.0
GPA and hoping for flexibility.** You're applying to a preparatory program
whose entire purpose is admitting people in exactly your position, and the
pass mark on *that* program — not your original undergraduate GPA — is
what determines whether you progress.

Kaplan states this outright: it's designed for people who **"don't quite
meet the entry requirements to start a postgraduate degree at a UK
university directly."** That is precisely your situation, named as the
target audience, not a gap you're hoping gets overlooked.

# What to actually check before committing to one

**The subject-specific progression table, not just the university name.**
A real, current warning worth repeating exactly: a pathway linked to a
university's *Business Management* pre-master's does not automatically
grant you entry into that same university's *Data Science* or
*Statistics* master's — progression is usually tied to a specific named
degree or a narrow group of related degrees. Confirm the exact target
master's program is named in your pathway's progression table **before**
paying any deposit, not after.

**The required pass grade on the pre-master's itself.** "Guaranteed"
progression is real, but it's conditional — typically on achieving an
overall average (often somewhere in the 50–60% range depending on the
provider and course) plus acceptable attendance. This is a real bar you
have to clear, just a substantially lower and more achievable one than
direct master's entry.

**Duration and cost as one combined package**, not two separate budgets.
Pre-master's programs commonly run 2–3 terms (roughly 3–9 months
depending on your starting point), and the total realistic cost is
pre-master's tuition **plus** the master's tuition that follows — treat
this as one number when comparing against a direct-entry route.

**English language requirement**, since this is separate from your
academic grade and still needs to be met — pathway providers often bundle
a pre-sessional English component if your IELTS/English score isn't yet
sufficient, but confirm what's included versus what's charged separately.

**SOAS direct online Pre-Master's** — worth checking whether their

pathways at other universities; worth comparing their specific Data
Science / Statistics progression tables directly against Kaplan's before
choosing a provider, since the right provider is decided by which
university and degree you actually want, not by the provider's brand.

# The one thing to do before applying anywhere

Contact the pathway provider (or the university's own international
office, for a direct program) and ask explicitly: **"Does your
Pre-Master's in [subject] guarantee progression into [exact master's
program name], and what is the required pass grade and attendance
threshold?"** Get that answer in writing before paying a deposit. The
counselor guidance findable on current pathway sites explicitly warns
against assuming a general pathway covers a specific master's — this is
the single most common, and most costly, mistake people make with this
route.
